Compounding as Illustrative Math

Learner target: Explain compounding and identify what an assumed-rate illustration cannot prove.

Education only. No real-money exercise is required. Never enter a real recovery phrase, private key, wallet password, or exchange login into a lesson or form.

Key ideas

  • Compounding describes growth being applied to a changing base over repeated periods.
  • It is a mathematical relationship, not evidence that a particular asset will deliver a stated rate.
  • A calculator using an assumed rate illustrates the assumption; it does not prove the real-world return.

Example

With an assumed 10% rate, $100 becomes $110 after one period. The next period’s 10% growth is calculated on $110, not the original $100.

Try it

In one sentence, what does compounding mean here? Then explain what a calculator assuming 5% annual growth has and has not proven.
